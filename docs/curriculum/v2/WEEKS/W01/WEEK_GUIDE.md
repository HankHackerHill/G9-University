# G9 W01 — Linux, KVM isolation, shell and systems fundamentals

**Purpose.** Establish, in five days, the Linux privilege model and the habits that every later week depends on: who may do what and why (identity, path resolution, mode bits, ACLs, capabilities, sudo delegation, mandatory access control), what a service is and how it differs from its configuration (systemd state, sockets, logs, SSH daemon settings), how to write a safe inventory tool in Bash and Python, and how to prove that a lab is isolated instead of naming it so. The week ends with a scored Friday: a closed-book knowledge exam and a blind Linux hardening or troubleshooting practical. Output of the week: `W01-Linux-Baseline-and-Hardening.md`. Every command in the week is proposed text for the owner's review; nothing was run to write it, and the local lab does not exist while Stage 00 is blocked.

**Lab stack.** Local, once Stage 00 is resolved and the owner has approved each step: `G9-ATK-01` (RED `10.77.10.10`) and `G9-TGT-01` (DMZ `10.77.20.20`) on separate isolated networks, with `G9-RTR-01` only for the optional cross-zone step. Configured allocation of the week's stack: 8 vCPU, 11 GiB, 140 GiB thin, at most 35 percent of the 31 GiB budget. The toy service `g9toy_reportd.py` runs inside `G9-TGT-01` only. While Stage 00 is blocked nothing local is assumed: the week runs on first-day systems theory, the owner's read-only host diagnostics, synthetic evidence in `LABS/fixtures/`, a toy directory in the owner's evidence folder, hosted OverTheWire Bandit, and a paper topology; every substitution is recorded and the isolation gate is marked as a blocker.

**Competencies.** `lab.authorization` (day 1), `linux.filesystem` (day 2), `linux.processes-services` and `linux.privilege-boundaries` (day 3), `python.secure-parsing` and `python.testing-fixtures` (day 4), `linux.evidence-completeness`, `linux.hardening-baseline` and `linux.blind-troubleshooting` (day 5, with the Friday practical at the band of `linux.blind-troubleshooting`). The packs are `linux-python-systems` and, for `linux.privilege-boundaries`, `windows-identity-ad`. A hinted result counts as guided evidence, not independent evidence.

**Accelerator.** Optional, never counted in the 480 hours and dropped without breaking the week. For a learner whose entry diagnostic is at least 90 percent on the Linux domain and who passes an independent practical, the redundant introduction of days 1 and 2 is replaced by one of: reading the capability masks of `/proc/<pid>/status` against `capabilities(7)` and asking which capability would make a given denial disappear; designing, on paper, an audit watch for the toy report directory and the negative case that proves it does not fire on the service's own reads; driving the unit of the toy service to a target exposure value with `systemd-analyze security` and stating, setting by setting, what the number does not prove; reading the AppArmor profiles that confine the lab's own guests, read-only; further modules of the Linux Luminarium. Each extension ends in a record in the format of `docs/curriculum/v2/TEMPLATES/LAB_RECORD.md`.

## Goals

Each goal is an observable outcome with the artifact that shows it (`EVIDENCE_CHECKLIST.md` gives the pass or fail test of each).

1. Decide an access request from a listing: name the class the kernel chose (owner, group or other), the directory element that must grant search, the ACL entry and mask, the mount or mandatory-access layer, and the identity the process actually holds, for the twelve and nine requests of the two drills. Artifact: the permission decision table. [linux.filesystem]
2. Separate the facts a service has: enabled or active, written value or effective value, who listens or who owns the listener, what the journal says or leaves out. Artifact: the annotated logs and the effective-value table. [linux.processes-services]
3. Write a scope line, a rules-of-engagement draft and an approval trail before acting, and keep every host command read-only and individually approved. Artifact: scope lines, Stage 00 record. [lab.authorization]
4. Judge an isolation claim from evidence: interfaces and MACs, routes, host captures that contain the stimulus, firewall and router logs, IPv6; report PASS, FAIL, INCONCLUSIVE or NOT RUN per test; or mark a blocker with no intrusive local testing. Artifact: the isolation record or the blocker line. [lab.authorization, linux.evidence-completeness]
5. Reproduce one permission or service misconfiguration on an authorized target, state its cause at the control, correct it with the least privilege that works, and show a positive and a near-miss negative retest. Artifact: a lab record at the status the evidence supports. [linux.hardening-baseline, linux.privilege-boundaries]
6. Assess a service and an SSH daemon against a written policy in a context you have not seen in the guided lab. Artifact: the independent-lab record. [linux.processes-services, linux.privilege-boundaries]
7. Build, without a reference solution, an inventory of accounts, processes, services and listeners plus a failed-authentication log parser, in Python or Bash, with safe subprocess use, JSON output, exception handling and at least eight tests of which five are negative. Artifact: source, tests, saved run. [python.secure-parsing, python.testing-fixtures]
8. Work the Bandit levels 0 to 15 without a published solution and keep a record per level that explains the access mechanism in your words and holds no password. Artifact: the two Bandit records. [linux.filesystem]
9. Take the Friday blind scenario: scope first, enumerate with unprivileged access, state what that access cannot show, hypothesize, test, correct, retest, and leave a baseline note another person can verify. Artifact: the Friday bundle and `W01-Linux-Baseline-and-Hardening.md`. [linux.blind-troubleshooting, linux.hardening-baseline]
10. State what the week did not prove: LAB TESTED stays NO until the owner's runs are recorded; a hosted or paper result is never reported as a local one. Artifact: the unresolved-blockers list and the substitution record.

## Risks and safety

- Stage 00 is BLOCKED. The missing libvirt socket is a root-cause question, never permission to install, enable or restart anything. A connection to a socket-activated daemon can start it: it is class C and needs its own approval.
- The trusted host is never a target. Guests never get a route to the household LAN or the Internet; a network name, a VPN kill switch or an XML definition is never offered as proof of containment. Isolation is a measured property of a stack and a policy version.
- The deliberately weak states of the week (a group-writable report, a wildcard delegation, an SSH setting that is not the one written, a secret in a unit) exist only inside disposable guests, are exercised only over loopback or with `sudo -u` from the administrator account, use synthetic files and synthetic accounts, and no password is ever set or guessed. An SSH probe sends no credential.
- No third-party target exists in the week. Hosted work is limited to the exercise a scope line names, on the platform's terms read and dated. No Bandit password, flag, solution step or screenshot of any active exercise enters the portfolio.
- A provisioning file refuses to run unless the guest carries its marker file; they are guards against a slip, not security boundaries. Run them only inside the named guest.
- A host-side capture can hold real traffic of the owner's network; it stays in the private evidence folder and is redacted before any sharing.
- Do not read the independent-lab provisioning file, the Friday provisioning file or the Friday bundle before their attempt: their hashes in the manifest are the commitment.
- Stop conditions STOP-01 to STOP-16 apply to every exercise. A safety failure or an evidence-integrity failure is never a passed practical, whatever the score.
